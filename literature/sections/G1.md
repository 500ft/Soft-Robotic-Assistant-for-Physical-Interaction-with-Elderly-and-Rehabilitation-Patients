# G1 — Patent prior art: verification pass

**Scope:** independent verification of eight patent numbers supplied by an LLM for the prior-art section of a *freestanding compliant soft-robotic upper body (torso + two arms)* for elderly and rehabilitation patients.

**Verification rule used:** every number below was checked by fetching the patent page itself, not a search snippet. Where Google Patents' own bibliographic fields disagree with the issuing office's front page, the **office front page wins** and both are shown. Nothing here is filled in from memory. Verified 2026-09-24.

**Result in one line:** all eight numbers exist and all eight are real documents in the claimed subject area — **no fabrications**. Two entries carry wrong assignee metadata if copied from Google Patents, and three carry date or wording errors. Details below.

### Bioliberty soft robotic assistive device — Bioliberty Ltd, 2022 (priority) / 2024 (WO) / 2025 (GB grant)

- **Citation:** GB2622575B and WO2024052578A1, "Soft robotic assistive device". Applicant: **Bioliberty Ltd** (Edinburgh, UK). Inventors: **Shéa Quinn, Conan Bradley, Ross O'Hanlon, Rowan Armstrong**. Priority: **2022-09-11** (GB application 2213268.2). PCT: PCT/EP2023/074940 filed 2023-09-11, published **WO2024052578A1 on 2024-03-14**. GB B-specification published (granted) **2025-01-08**; anticipated expiry 2042-09-11. Family also includes EP4583989A1 and US20260077229A1.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/GB2622575B/en and https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024052578A1/en
- **Open access:** yes. Note: the **GB-B page is bibliographic only** — Google Patents hosts no full text or abstract for the GB grant (fetched page contains zero occurrences of "piezo"). All technical content below was read off the **WO2024052578A1** full text, which is the same disclosure.
- **What it shows:** A soft pneumatic actuator system that actuates, assists or resists movement of a human body part (primarily a hand/finger glove), driven by a **piezoelectric resonant acoustic gas pump** — verbatim: "a piezoelectric resonant acoustic gas pump (or a gas pump that is configured to generate and rectify an acoustic standing wave to provide gas flow)" — in place of a conventional compressor. The system uses "pressure measurement or measurements to estimate any one or any combination of: (a) the volume of the internal chamber of the soft pneumatic actuator; (b) the three-dimensional position, shape, and/or displacement of the soft pneumatic actuator; and (c) the force (e.g. torque) delivered by the soft pneumatic actuator."
- **Hard numbers:** pump peak pressure **at least 100 mbar(g)** (pref. ≥200, ≥300 mbar(g)) up to **at most 2 bar(g)**; peak vacuum **at least −100 mbar(g)** (pref. −150); peak flow **0.1–5 L/min** (pref. 0.5–4, e.g. 1.5–2 L/min); drive frequency **18–25 kHz**, preferably 20–22 kHz; pump internal cavity volume **0.1–2 cm³**, preferably 0.2–1 cm³.
- **Design relevance:** This is the single most constraining document on the list, and the constraint is **not** the actuator — it is the **sensing method**. Estimating soft-actuator shape, displacement, volume and delivered force from chamber pressure alone is exactly the sensorless-proprioception route a freestanding soft arm wants, and it is now granted in GB with EP and US pending. If the project plans "we infer arm pose and contact force from actuator pressure," read these claims first. The resonant-pump claim is a second, narrower fence around silent/compact onboard air supply.
- **Evidence grade:** P-VERIFIED
- **Limitations:** **Wearable** — the disclosure is a body-mounted glove/limb device, not a freestanding robot. Jurisdiction: **GB granted**; WO is a publication only; EP/US still pending. The claims are written around the actuator + pump + estimation method, which is scale-agnostic and therefore reaches further than the wearable embodiment suggests.

### HKU soft robotic glove — University of Hong Kong (Hu, Wen & Wang), 2019

- **Citation:** WO2019223568A1, "Soft robotic glove for rehabilitation". Applicant: **The University of Hong Kong**. Inventors: **Yong Hu, Rongwei Wen, Zheng Wang**. Priority **2018-05-23**; international application **PCT/CN2019/086725** filed **2019-05-14**; published **2019-11-28**.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2019223568A1/en
- **Open access:** yes, full text readable.
- **What it shows:** A wearable rehabilitation glove in which a wrist-mounted soft pneumatic actuator (dual polymer tubes with opposed pistons and an internal wire rope) extends or retracts a **flexible polymer rod**; a fabric sleeve over the rod, tied to the glove by adjustable "constrained lines," transmits that motion to the fingertip to produce finger flexion and extension.
- **Hard numbers:** none reported — compressed-air actuation is claimed with no pressure, force or dimensional values in the published text.
- **Design relevance:** Informative only. It is the canonical "rigid-rod-plus-fabric-constraint" trick for turning one linear soft actuator into a controlled joint trajectory, which is a cheap way to get a defined arc out of an undefined soft element. It blocks nothing for a torso-scale robot.
- **Evidence grade:** P-VERIFIED
- **Limitations:** **Wearable** (glove). This is a **PCT publication only** — not a granted patent in any jurisdiction as shown on this page; do not describe it as "patented."

### Shanghai University pneumatic wearable upper-limb system — Shanghai University (Ma, Lei, Zhai & Yu), 2019/2020

- **Citation:** CN110812124A, 一种气动驱动的柔性穿戴式上肢康复系统 — "Pneumatically driven flexible wearable upper limb rehabilitation system". Applicant per CNIPA front page (field 71): **上海大学 / Shanghai University**, Shanghai 200444. Inventors: **马新愿 Ma Xinyuan, 雷静桃 Lei Jingtao, 翟宇毅 Zhai Yuyi, 于琳 Yu Lin**. Application CN201911237501.0 filed **2019-12-06**; A-publication **2020-02-21**; **granted 2021-12-03** (CN110812124B). Google Patents currently lists the status as fee-related/expired.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/CN110812124A/en and the CNIPA front page in the official PDF, https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/31/c4/d5/9faa9516893b72/CN110812124A.pdf
- **Open access:** yes. English machine translation readable; Chinese original PDF fetched to resolve the assignee.
- **What it shows:** A three-module fabric exosuit (shoulder assembly, upper-arm assembly, forearm/wrist assembly) joined by straps and hook-and-loop, in which pneumatic artificial muscles pull **Bowden cables** to drive elbow flexion/extension, forearm rotation and wrist flexion/extension. sEMG sensors read user intent; superelastic pressure sensors on the inner faces of the straps read interface contact pressure; encoders read position.
- **Hard numbers:** **five pneumatic artificial muscles** (items 23, 24, 25, 27, 28) — **three at the biceps position, two at the triceps position**; cable length adjustable per wearer via a thread adjuster (chuck 19-5 with four projecting posts). No pressures, forces or masses are quantified anywhere in the claims or description.
- **Design relevance:** Useful as evidence that PAM + Bowden routing is an accepted way to get multi-DOF arm motion without rigid linkages, and as a reminder that **interface pressure sensing at every strap** is the standard answer to "how do you know you are hurting the patient." Blocks nothing for a freestanding machine.
- **Evidence grade:** P-PARTIAL
- **What differs from the claimed entry:** (a) the claimed year "(2020)" is the **A-publication** year — the filing is **2019-12-06**; (b) **Google Patents lists the assignee as "Beijing Transpacific Technology Development Ltd," which is wrong** — that is a metadata artifact, and the CNIPA front page names **Shanghai University** as applicant (the agency of record is 上海上大专利事务所, agent 顾勇华). If this citation is copied from Google Patents into the report, the assignee will be wrong.
- **Limitations:** **Wearable** exosuit. **CN jurisdiction only**, granted 2021, now lapsed for fees per Google's status field.

### Henan Polytechnic shoulder circumduction robot — Henan Polytechnic University (Zhang et al.), 2020 filing / 2023 grant

- **Citation:** CN112263435B, 一种针对肩关节进行环转运动的康复机器人装置 — "Rehabilitation robot device for circumduction motion of the shoulder joint". Patentee per CNIPA front page (field 73): **河南理工大学 / Henan Polytechnic University**, Jiaozuo 454000. Inventors: **张业明 Zhang Yeming, 和双洋 He Shuangyang, 石岩 Shi Yan, 许未晴 Xu Weiqing, 蔡茂林 Cai Maolin, 刘俊磊 Liu Junlei, 李东园 Li Dongyuan, 郭春阁 Guo Chun'ge**. Application CN202011088267.2 filed **2020-10-13**; A-publication CN112263435A **2021-01-26**; **granted (B) 2023-03-31**; active, anticipated expiry 2040-10-13.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/CN112263435B/en and the CNIPA B-specification front page, https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/79/51/47/d12450284fd34e/CN112263435B.pdf
- **Open access:** yes; Chinese original fetched to resolve patentee and control detail.
- **What it shows:** A **freestanding** shoulder rehabilitation rig: trolleys driven by hub motors run on I-section arc guide rails; each trolley carries a winding drum whose rope is pulled by a **pneumatic artificial muscle**, and the combination of rail travel and rope take-up sweeps the patient's arm through lifting, vertical-plane paths, a lower semicircle, and a full circumduction ("annular track"). Air supply runs through **electro-pneumatic proportional valves** (电气比例阀) — verbatim, "第一电气比例阀是一种可以通过电气信号实现对气压/流量成比例输出的控制装置" ("the first electro-pneumatic proportional valve is a control device that produces pressure/flow output proportional to an electrical signal") — plus a directional valve, lubricator and silencer, with a second proportional valve controlling the PAM's internal pressure.
- **Hard numbers:** none reported — no pressures, forces, muscle contraction ratios or rail dimensions are given. The only quantified safety element is qualitative: **U-shaped hooks release when "rope traction force exceeds upper limb bearing capacity,"** preventing secondary injury, but no threshold value is stated.
- **Design relevance:** This is the closest thing on the list to the project's own architecture — soft (PAM) actuation, proportional pressure control, freestanding frame, patient's arm attached at a single interface. It is the right document to cite for "proportional-pressure PAM control of a patient's limb is prior art," and its mechanical-fuse hook is a design pattern worth copying and, unlike theirs, actually characterising with a number.
- **Evidence grade:** P-PARTIAL
- **What differs from the claimed entry:** (a) the claimed "(2020)" is the **filing** year; the B document published **2023-03-31**; (b) **Google Patents gives the assignee as "Henan University of Technology" — this is a mistranslation.** 河南理工大学 is **Henan Polytechnic University** (Jiaozuo); 河南工业大学 (Henan University of Technology) is a different institution in Zhengzhou; (c) the claimed "shoulder rotation" understates it — 环转 is **circumduction**, i.e. the compound conical sweep, which is a broader and harder motion than rotation; (d) "cables" is right but they are rope-and-winding-drum, and the guides are motorised trolleys on arc rails.
- **Limitations:** **Freestanding**, rail-mounted, single-limb, seated/standing patient. **CN jurisdiction only.** Granted and active.

### Neofect upper-limb rehabilitation robot — Neofect Co Ltd, 2020 (publication)

- **Citation:** US20200129365A1, "Robot for upper-limb rehabilitation". Applicant: **Neofect Co Ltd** (South Korea). Inventors: **Min Jae Park, Hyeon Jae Ju, Young Geun Choi**. Priority **2017-06-30** (KR); US application 16/729,071 filed **2019-12-27**; published **2020-04-30**; **granted as US11617700B2**, adjusted expiry 2040-03-04.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US20200129365A1/en
- **Open access:** yes, full text readable.
- **What it shows:** A single integrated rehabilitation robot with three co-operating sub-devices: a **finger exercise device** on the dorsum of the hand (motor-driven roller winding elastic straps to finger rings, with strain sensors reading finger force); a **wrist exercise device** (U-shaped cradle on a curved track); and an **upper-limb exercise device** at the elbow — a two-link arm with a spring-and-pulley network that **gravity-compensates** the limb so the patient exercises "without applying an excessive force."
- **Hard numbers:** none reported — no forces, torques, spring rates or workspace dimensions are given.
- **Design relevance:** The verified example of *integrating three joint scales into one machine*, which is the claim structure a bilateral whole-upper-body robot will bump into. Its real lesson is the gravity-compensation spring/pulley stage: the patent's own stated purpose is removing limb weight so the residual assistive force can be small, which is the cheapest way to hit a meaningful assist target without a large actuator.
- **Evidence grade:** P-VERIFIED
- **Limitations:** **Hybrid** — body-mounted cuffs, rings and cradles carried on a **frame-mounted, cantilevered** arm; the patient's limb is suspended and does not touch the floor. Single-limb. **US granted** (US11617700B2), so the published application number cited in the list is the pre-grant document; cite the grant if the point is enforceability.

### DGIST upper limb rehabilitation robot — Daegu Gyeongbuk Institute of Science and Technology (An et al.), 2014/2018

- **Citation:** US9956130B2, "Upper limb rehabilitation robot". Assignee: **Daegu Gyeongbuk Institute of Science and Technology (DGIST)**. Inventors: **Jinung An, Yoon Gu Kim, Gwang Hee Jang, Jung Hyun Choi**. Priority **2014-07-07** (KR); US application 14/558,745 filed **2014-12-03**; pre-grant publication US20160000633A1; **granted 2018-05-01**; active, adjusted expiry 2037-01-27.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US9956130B2/en
- **Open access:** yes, full text readable.
- **What it shows:** A base-frame machine with a laterally rotatable connecting support carrying a connecting shaft unit and a **four-rod link unit**; the patient's limb attaches at the joint between the third and fourth rods. An electric motor and gear train drive it actively; a **variable damper** "generates resistant torque against torque of the connecting shaft unit by adjusting a damper value" for resistive work; the same machine can be left unpowered for free limb movement. A motorised rack-and-pinion lifter sets working height, and reorienting the connecting support gives horizontal, inclined and vertical exercise planes.
- **Hard numbers:** none reported — no torques, damping coefficients, link lengths or workspace figures appear.
- **Design relevance:** The cleanest prior-art statement that *one frame + one linkage + a switchable damper covers the whole assist/resist mode space in three exercise planes*. If the project claims "our robot does assistive, active and resistive modes in multiple planes," that claim is already occupied at the system level — the project's novelty has to live in the compliance and the bilateral whole-body contact, not in the mode taxonomy.
- **Evidence grade:** P-PARTIAL
- **What differs from the claimed entry:** the modes are **passive / active / resistive**, not "assistant/active/resistive" — the unpowered mode is passive free movement by the user, and there is no separately claimed "assistant" mode. Also, "actuated linkage" is right but the specific claimed geometry is a **four-rod link unit** with the limb connector at the 3rd–4th rod joint, and resistance comes from a **variable damper**, not from the motor. The claimed "(2018)" is the **grant** year; the priority is 2014.
- **Limitations:** **Freestanding**, base-frame, height-adjustable, single-limb, no wearable components. **US granted and active.**

### CASIA five-bar upper limb rehabilitation system — Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Hou et al.), 2014/2020

- **Citation:** US10596056B2, "Upper limb rehabilitation robot system". Assignee: **Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences (CASIA)**. Inventors: **Zengguang Hou, Liang Peng, Weiqun Wang, Long Cheng, Guibin Bian, Xiaoliang Xie**. Priority/filing **2014-07-15** (national phase of PCT/CN2014/082270, US application 15/326,132); pre-grant publication US20170209327A1; **granted 2020-03-24**; active, adjusted expiry 2035-07-31.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US10596056B2/en
- **Open access:** yes, full text readable.
- **What it shows:** A computer plus a rehabilitation robot whose manipulator is a **two-DOF five-bar parallel planar mechanism** (four bars, two rotation axes) giving "force output in any direction of the motion in the planar workspace." Torque from an **electric machine and magnetic brake** is "magnified and transmitted to the kinematic mechanism by **cable transmission**" rather than direct drive, explicitly to shrink component size and cost. The patient grips a handheld terminal carrying a touch sensor and a vibration motor; a **multi-dimensional force/torque sensor** measures interaction force. Passive, active (virtual impedance) and hybrid modes, bilateral training, networked multi-patient operation, training records and visual feedback.

If the project's answer to "how do you know the assistive force" is a load cell at the hand, that is this patent's territory; the defensible difference has to be that the compliance is distributed along a soft limb rather than concentrated at a rigid endpoint.
- **Evidence grade:** P-VERIFIED
- **Limitations:** **Freestanding** tabletop unit; seated patient grips a handle; **planar 2-DOF only** — no out-of-plane or whole-body contact. **US granted and active.**

### Beri soft robotic assistive gripper — Alekh Rajesh Beri (individual), 2017/2021

- **Citation:** US11027436B2, "Soft robotic assistive gripping device". Inventor and assignee: **Alekh Rajesh Beri** (individual, unassigned). Priority **2017-06-19**; US application 15/958,744 filed **2018-04-20**; pre-grant publication US20180361596A1; **granted 2021-06-08**; active, adjusted expiry 2039-02-21.
- **DOI/URL:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US11027436B2/en

- **Hard numbers:** actuation pressure **under 5 psi** (photographs demonstrate gripping "with air pressure under 4 psi"); reported grip capability **up to 17,000 grams** (≈167 N); body silicone **Shore 00-30 to 30A**, skin layer **Shore 00-30 to 50A** and **about 2 mm** thick; internal air channels **1–2 mm**; fingertip micro-channels **100–500 μm**; flex sensors span **0°–180°**; named components include TMP36 temperature sensors and FlexiForce A301 pressure sensors; operating band cited as 15–37 °C, 1–25 psi, 0–180°.

## What this actually blocks

**Blocks nothing structurally — four of eight are wearables and are irrelevant to the freestanding novelty argument.** GB2622575B/WO2024052578A1, WO2019223568A1, CN110812124A and US11027436B2 are all body-mounted. A freestanding robot that contacts a patient externally does not read on a claim whose preamble requires the device to be worn on the body part it moves. Cite them as actuator and sensing art, not as architectural obstacles, and do not let the report imply the project is crowded by them.

**The one claim that genuinely bites is a method claim, not a device claim.** Bioliberty's pressure-based estimation of soft-actuator **volume, three-dimensional position/shape/displacement and delivered force** is scale-agnostic — nothing in it is confined to a glove. If the project's proprioception and force-estimation story is "we read chamber pressure and infer arm state and contact force," that is granted in GB and pending in EP and US with a 2022-09-11 priority. Either design around it (add an independent measurement — an IMU, a load cell, or optical/capacitive shape sensing that the estimate does not rely on pressure alone for), or state plainly in the report that the estimation method is prior art and the project's contribution is elsewhere. The resonant-pump claim is narrower and only matters if the design puts a piezoelectric acoustic pump onboard.

**The four freestanding entries constrain the vocabulary, not the machine.** CN112263435B, US20200129365A1, US9956130B2 and US10596056B2 collectively already own: assist/active/resist mode switching on a frame-mounted rehab robot (US9956130B2); endpoint force control with a multi-axis F/T sensor plus training records and visual feedback (US10596056B2); integrating finger, wrist and arm scales into one machine with gravity compensation (US20200129365A1); and proportional-pressure PAM control pulling a patient's limb on a freestanding rig (CN112263435B). What none of them has is a **compliant, bilateral, whole-upper-body** robot: every one is single-limb, every one constrains the limb to a fixed track or planar workspace, none claims a soft torso, and none contemplates uncontrolled whole-body contact. That is where the project's novelty has to be argued, and it survives all eight.

**Practical warning for the report:** CN112263435B is the closest architectural match and is **granted and active in China until 2040**. If the project ever has a commercial path in China, its PAM-plus-proportional-valve limb-driving arrangement needs a freedom-to-operate look by someone qualified. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Adjacent patents I found that the list missed

All four below were found by searching Google Patents directly and were verified by fetching their patent pages. They are the freestanding patient-support and transfer art the original list omitted entirely — which matters, because this is the category the project actually competes in.

- **CN111166588A / CN111166588B — "Human body lifting and moving robot"**, Zhongqi Jiaxing Intelligent Technology Co Ltd; inventors 尹春晖, 李育良, 梁刘义; filed **2020-03-23**, published **2020-05-19**, granted. https://patents.google.com/patent/CN111166588A/en — An AGV chassis (100) carrying a load-bearing rotating shaft mechanism (200), a lifting mechanism (300) and **rake-type support arms (400)** with an intelligent control system (500), explicitly for moving patients between stretcher, bed, operating table and imaging equipment in hospitals and care homes. **Freestanding, mobile.** The direct commercial competitor architecture: rigid rake arms and a lifting column, no compliance anywhere. Cite it as the incumbent the project's compliance argument is aimed at.

- **JP5195569B2 — "Transfer device, transfer method, and holder"**, **Toyota Motor Corporation**; inventor 山内洋司; filed **2009-03-26**, granted **2013-05-08**, active. https://patents.google.com/patent/JP5195569B2/en — Toyota's patient-transfer device and body holder. Worth citing because it establishes that a major manufacturer took the rigid-holder route to patient transfer more than fifteen years ago, which is the strongest available argument that the unsolved part is the *interface compliance*, not the lifting.
