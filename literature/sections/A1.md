# A1 — Soft and compliant actuation capable of meaningful assistive torque

**Design decision:** What actuates the arm? Can any genuinely soft technology deliver
useful force at human scale — order 10–100 N at the end effector, 10–40 Nm at the shoulder
against gravity?

**Method note:** 15 searches run across Google Scholar surface, arXiv, IEEE/Science/Wiley
publisher pages, PMC, MDPI and Frontiers. Every entry below was verified by fetching either
the Semantic Scholar Graph API record for its DOI, a PMC full-text page, an arXiv abs/HTML
page, or the publisher page. The fetched verification source is named in each entry. Papers
I could not verify are not listed; unverified leads are flagged at the bottom.

### HASEL-vs-muscle benchmark table — Rothemund et al., 2021

- **Citation:** Rothemund, P., Kellaris, N., Mitchell, S. K., Acome, E., Keplinger, C. (2021).
  "HASEL Artificial Muscles for a New Generation of Lifelike Robots — Recent Progress and
  Future Opportunities." *Advanced Materials* 33, 2003375. (Online 2020.)
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202003375 — verified via PMC full text
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11469257/) and Semantic Scholar DOI record.
- **Open access:** yes (CC-BY, PMC11469257)
- **What it shows:** The authoritative side-by-side table of HASEL electrohydraulic artificial
  muscle performance against natural skeletal muscle, written by the group that invented the
  technology. It is the single best source for "how close is the best electrostatic soft
  actuator to muscle, honestly."
- **Hard numbers:** Natural muscle: 0.35 MPa stress, 20% typical / 40% max strain, 50 W/kg avg
  and 200 W/kg peak specific power, 8 J/kg typical / 40 J/kg max specific energy, 40% efficiency,
  500 %/s peak strain rate, ~10⁹ cycle life. HASEL: 0.3 MPa (planar), 0.21 MPa (Peano),
  0.09 MPa (HS-Peano); strain up to 124% (planar) / 118% (quadrant donut) / 24% (HS-Peano);
  614 W/kg at resonance (planar), 360 W/kg peak (Peano); 70 J/kg at resonance; 19–21% efficiency;
  126 Hz bandwidth (quadrant donut), 7400 %/s peak strain rate; drive voltages 10 kV (Peano),
  12 kV (quadrant donut), 22.5 kV (planar); cycle life 10⁶ (elastomeric) and 10⁴–10⁵ (thermoplastic).
- **Design relevance:** Sets the ceiling. HASEL matches muscle in *stress* (0.3 vs 0.35 MPa) and
  beats it in specific power, but that stress is only achievable over tiny cross-sections at
  10–22 kV. It tells the team that the electroactive family is not stress-limited — it is
  cross-section, voltage and packaging limited.
- **Evidence grade:** D (review by the originating lab; underlying numbers are B)
- **Limitations:** Every number is per-actuator on a bench. Nothing here addresses carrying a
  static gravity load for minutes, backdriving under a 700 N leaning adult, or the 10–22 kV
  supply sitting inside a torso that a frail person touches. Cycle life of 10⁴–10⁵ for the
  thermoplastic variants is a few weeks of assistive duty at best.

### Electrohydraulic force/strain census — Tynan et al., 2025

- **Citation:** Tynan, L., Gunawardana, U., Liyanapathirana, R., Perera, O., Esposito, D.,
  Centracchio, J., Gargiulo, G. (2025). "Review of Electrohydraulic Actuators Inspired by the
  HASEL Actuator." *Biomimetics* 10(3), 152.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/biomimetics10030152 — verified via PMC full text
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11939893/).
- **Open access:** yes (MDPI, CC-BY)
- **What it shows:** A complete tabulated census of every published electrohydraulic /
  HASEL-derived actuator variant with force, strain, voltage and specific power. This is the
  "how much force has anyone ever actually gotten out of one of these" table.
- **Hard numbers:** *Expanding:* elastomeric donut HASEL 2.45–3.92 N @ 21 kV, 40–50% strain;
  planar HASEL 2.45–14.72 N @ ~22.5 kV, 79% strain, 614 W/kg, 70 J/kg; quadrant HASEL
  (3-stack) ~60 N @ 12 kV, 118% strain, 121 W/kg, 12 J/kg; HEXEL <2 N; HAXEL 0.1–0.8 N @ 2 kV;
  LEAP 0.017 N. *Contracting:* Peano-HASEL (3-stack) 9.81–60 N @ 10 kV, 9–15% strain, 160 W/kg;
  HS Peano-HASEL 18 N @ 10 kV, 24% strain; HEXEL (contracting) 37.6 N @ 9.5 kV; EBM (6-series)
  ~7 N; electro-ribbon 0.172 N; EPP-BAM 0.981 N; HALVE 5 N @ 1.1 kV, 50.5 W/kg. Authors'
  conclusion quoted: *"low force and displacement still limit the viability of the soft
  actuators in real-life applications."*
- **Design relevance:** Kills the electroactive family for this project outright. The maximum
  force ever reported from any electrohydraulic actuator is ~60 N from a multi-unit stack at
  12 kV — that is the whole end-effector target, from an actuator that then has to be
  multiplied by a moment arm. There is no path from this table to 10–40 Nm at a shoulder.
- **Evidence grade:** D (review; sources are B)
- **Limitations:** Review of bench characterisations only; no assembled limb, no sustained
  load-holding data, no contact-safety data.

### Soft shoulder actuator, measured at the joint — Zhang et al., 2024

- **Citation:** Zhang, H., Naquila, G. J., Bae, J., Wu, Z.-H., Hingwe, A., Deshpande, A. D.
  (2024). "Novel bio-inspired soft actuators for upper-limb exoskeletons: design, fabrication
  and feasibility study." *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* 11, 1451231.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2024.1451231 — verified via Frontiers full text and
  Semantic Scholar DOI record. Preprint mirror: arXiv:2509.01145.
- **Open access:** yes (CC-BY)
- **What it shows:** Two purpose-built silicone pneumatic actuators — LISPER (elbow) and SCASPER
  (shoulder) — characterised for torque, ROM and bandwidth, with an analytical pressure→torque
  model. The authors explicitly built SCASPER because LISPER's output was too weak for the
  shoulder, and published both numbers.
- **Hard numbers:** LISPER (elbow): 12.5 N max output force, **0.6 Nm max torque**, 112.2° ROM,
  100 kPa max, 259 g, 132×55×92 mm. SCASPER (shoulder): 49.5 N max output force, **5.5 Nm max
  torque**, 122.5° ROM, 150 kPa max, 183 g, 122×91×132 mm. Bellows contribute ~35% of total
  output force. Authors state the LISPER "output force … is insufficient to support motions
  of shoulder."
- **Design relevance:** This is the most direct measurement of the gap in the whole set. A
  purpose-designed, state-of-the-art, fully soft silicone shoulder actuator produces 5.5 Nm —
  roughly one seventh of the low end of a 10–40 Nm shoulder requirement, and it does so with no
  structural load path of its own.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated; "preliminary test on a dummy arm", no humans)
- **Limitations:** Wearable actuator mounted on a human skeleton that carries the load; the
  torque number is assistive torque added to a limb, not torque a freestanding structure must
  generate and react. Dummy-arm testing only. No durability or contact-safety data.

### Dual-DOF soft shoulder exosuit with human trial — Chen et al., 2026

- **Citation:** Chen, R., Chiaradia, D., Leonardis, D., Frisoli, A. (2026). "Design and
  Characterization of a Dual-DOF Soft Shoulder Exosuit with Volume-Optimized Pneumatic
  Actuator." arXiv preprint arXiv:2602.18212.
- **DOI/URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18212 — verified by fetching both the arXiv abs page
  and the full HTML (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18212).
- **Open access:** yes (arXiv preprint; peer-review status unconfirmed)
- **What it shows:** A two-actuator soft pneumatic shoulder exosuit (curved abduction actuator +
  horizontal adduction actuator) with a geometry optimisation that cuts actuator volume while
  keeping torque, plus a 10-subject EMG study. It is currently the highest joint-level torque
  reported for a *fully soft pneumatic* shoulder actuator that has been on real people.
- **Hard numbers:** CAA max moment **9.7 Nm at ~90° abduction, 90 kPa**. HAA modelled max torque
  **10.93 Nm at β=60°, 90 kPa**, falling to 4.64 Nm at β=120°. Operating pressures swept
  10–90 kPa; demonstrations at 80 kPa (CAA) and 50 kPa (HAA). Actuator volume 357 mL vs 555 mL
  baseline (−35.7%) at 94.2% torque retention; fall time 35.2% faster at 90 kPa. Wearable mass
  390 g. Ten healthy subjects (5 M / 5 F, 27.1 ± 3.0 y); EMG reduction up to 59% (abduction,
  HAA only) and 63.7% (flexion, dual actuator) across seven muscles.
- **Design relevance:** This is the practical ceiling number to quote in the design review:
  ~10 Nm per soft pneumatic shoulder actuator at 90 kPa. It lands at the very bottom of the
  10–40 Nm band and only with a tuned geometry, a tether, and a human skeleton underneath.
- **Evidence grade:** A (hardware, 10 human subjects, EMG outcome measures) — with the caveat
  that this is a preprint.
- **Limitations:** Exosuit, not a freestanding arm: the torque is reacted through the wearer's
  torso. Healthy young subjects, not elderly or rehab patients. Tethered pneumatics.
  No leaning/impact load case. Preprint, unreviewed.

### Soft pneumatic bundles — highest verified torque from an all-soft module — Robertson et al., 2017

- **Citation:** Robertson, M. A., Sadeghi, H., Florez, J. M., Paik, J. (2017). "Soft Pneumatic
  Actuator Fascicles for High Force and Reliability." *Soft Robotics* 4(1), 23–32.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1089/soro.2016.0029 — verified via PMC full text
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5346960/).
- **Open access:** yes (PMC5346960)
- **What it shows:** Bundling multiple thin fiber-reinforced soft pneumatic actuators into a
  "fascicle" beats a single actuator of equal volume on force, and scales to a multi-DOF
  platform. It is the clearest demonstration that soft pneumatics scale by *parallelism*, not
  by inflating one actuator bigger.
- **Hard numbers:** Single SPA 26 N blocked force, 28 g, 145 mm × 17 mm, ~2 Hz bandwidth.
  4Pack fascicle **112 N blocked force**, 185 g, ~0.61 N/g, ~2 Hz. Full platform 945 g,
  **468 N total force capacity, 18 N·m maximum moment**, 1.33 Hz bandwidth. Operating pressure
  200 kPa gauge (2 bar max). Fiber angle 75.2°, 16 wraps. Fascicle gains 23% force over an
  equivalent single SPA.
- **Design relevance:** The 18 Nm figure is the single strongest "yes it can" data point in this
  literature, and it is the number to defend a soft pneumatic shoulder with. It also fixes the
  scaling law for the design: to double torque, double the number of actuators and the mass and
  the air flow. Bandwidth of 1.3–2 Hz sets the control ceiling for D1.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no humans)
- **Limitations:** A bending platform, not a revolute shoulder — the 18 Nm is a bending moment
  over a short structure, with unknown behaviour under an off-axis leaning load. 2007-era
  silicone, no fatigue data at load. 200 kPa plumbing plus compressor is a large fraction of a
  freestanding torso's mass and noise budget, and 1.33 Hz is slow for reacting to a fall.

### Soft-rigid hybrid joint, the realistic fallback — Dimonte et al., 2026

- **Citation:** Dimonte, G., Gandolla, M., Proietti, T. (2026). "A bioinspired hybrid robotic
  joint for safer physical human–robot interaction." *Bioinspiration & Biomimetics* 21(4),
  046004.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-3190/ae72db — verified via IOPscience article page.
- **Open access:** partial (IOP; check institutional access)
- **What it shows:** A rigid frame driven by an antagonistic pair of thermoplastic-polyurethane
  pneumatic artificial muscles, with two model-free controllers that regulate joint angle and
  joint compliance independently. Demonstrates that you can keep a tuneable-compliance,
  human-safe joint while getting force levels a fully soft link cannot reach.
- **Hard numbers:** PAM isometric force **400 N at 240 kPa**; stroke/stretchability 80 mm;
  material density 0.3 g/cm³. Position control <2° RMSE, <1% overshoot, rise time <1.3 s.
  **Stiffness modulation range 0.054–0.076 Nm/deg** (≈3.1–4.4 Nm/rad).
- **Design relevance:** This is the architecture the project probably ends up with. 400 N of
  muscle force on a rigid moment arm of 50–100 mm gives 20–40 Nm directly — the target band —
  while antagonistic pressure still gives real, controllable compliance. It also supplies a
  measured stiffness range to quote in the safety case.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no human subjects)
- **Limitations:** Single joint on a bench, not a two-arm torso. The stiffness modulation range
  is narrow (about 40% span) — it will not go floppy on demand. 240 kPa PAMs mean a compressor,
  valves and a plumbing failure mode. No impact or leaning-load testing, and the rigid frame
  reintroduces exactly the hard edges and pinch points the "soft" premise is trying to avoid.

### The honesty framing — Hawkes, Majidi & Tolley, 2021

- **Citation:** Hawkes, E. W., Majidi, C., Tolley, M. T. (2021). "Hard questions for soft
  robotics." *Science Robotics* 6, eabg6049.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.abg6049 — verified via the Semantic Scholar
  Graph API DOI record (publisher page returns 403 to automated fetch).
- **Open access:** no (CLOSED per Unpaywall)
- **What it shows:** Three senior figures in the field argue that soft robotics has been coasting
  on "soft for soft's sake" and propose a three-tier test for whether a soft solution actually
  contributes: does softness enable something otherwise impossible, or is it decoration?
- **Hard numbers:** none reported (position piece).
- **Design relevance:** This is the paper to cite in the design review when justifying a
  soft-rigid hybrid over a fully soft arm. It gives the team permission — from the field's own
  leadership — to say "we went soft only where softness buys safety, and rigid where it buys
  torque," and it is the strongest defence against a reviewer asking why the arm isn't
  monolithically soft.
- **Evidence grade:** D (review / position)
- **Limitations:** No data. Purely a framing device.

### Untethered soft pneumatic arm, whole-system numbers — Zuo et al., 2025

- **Citation:** Zuo, R., Han, D. H., Li, R., Jamal, S., Bruder, D. (2025). "UMArm: Untethered,
  Modular, Portable, Soft Pneumatic Arm." arXiv preprint arXiv:2505.11476 (v2, Dec 2025).
- **DOI/URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11476 — verified via arXiv abs page and full HTML
  (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11476v2).
- **Open access:** yes (arXiv preprint; peer-review status unconfirmed)
- **What it shows:** A complete untethered soft arm: 24 valve-embedded self-regulated McKibben
  actuators, no external pressure regulators, directionally tunable compliance, demonstrated as
  a wearable assistive arm. It is the closest published system to "a soft arm you could bolt
  to a torso and run off-grid."
- **Hard numbers:** **53.3 N blocked force at 1.15 kg arm mass** (regulators included).
  Max tested payload 2 kg. Input pressure 276 kPa (40 psi). 3 segments, 12 revolute joints,
  ±0.3 rad (±17.2°) per joint. 24 independently controlled McKibben actuators (8/segment).
  Antagonistic stiffness settings 14 / 55 / 110 kPa. Steady-state positional RMSE 0.85 mm at
  zero load, 110 kPa, 1× gain. Step settling 2–6 s depending on load and stiffness.
  Untethered runtime 11 min 45 s from a 1.11 L tank at 31 MPa.
- **Design relevance:** Gives the team a system-level reality check that bench actuator specs
  never do: the *entire* soft arm produces 53 N — mid-band for the 10–100 N end-effector target —
  and runs for under 12 minutes untethered on a 310 bar tank. For a freestanding assistant, this
  says: plan for a tethered compressor, not bottled gas.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no human-subject study)
- **Limitations:** Preprint. ±17° per joint is a small range. 2–6 s settling is far too slow to
  catch a stumbling person. No contact-safety or leaning-load characterisation. Runtime number
  effectively rules out an untethered pneumatic torso.

### Electrohydraulic joint torque, measured — Kellaris et al., 2021

- **Citation:** Kellaris, N., Rothemund, P., Zeng, Y., Mitchell, S. K., Smith, G. M.,
  Jayaram, K., Keplinger, C. (2021). "Spider-Inspired Electrohydraulic Actuators for Fast,
  Soft-Actuated Joints." *Advanced Science* 8(14), 2100916.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202100916 — verified via PMC full text
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8292915/).
- **Open access:** yes (CC-BY, PMC8292915)
- **What it shows:** The best-performing electrohydraulic *rotary joint* published — a
  spider-inspired soft joint combining Peano-HASEL and pouch-motor principles, characterised
  directly in torque rather than linear force.
- **Hard numbers:** **Blocked torque up to 70 mN·m.** Specific torque up to 21.2 N·m/kg
  (actuator mass only). Roll-off frequency up to 24 Hz. 6–9 kV DC. Actuator mass 1.36 g
  (2×4×1 cm). 70° range at 9 kV, ~180° in series. Specific power up to 230 W/kg. Rise time
  12 ms, fall time 31 ms. >2000 cycles with <3% degradation. Holding power <1 mW vs 140 mW for
  a comparable servo.
- **Design relevance:** The decisive negative result for the electroactive route. 70 mN·m is
  **~150–570× short** of a 10–40 Nm shoulder. The 21.2 N·m/kg specific torque is genuinely
  servo-class, but only if you ignore the kV supply mass; scaling the actuator mass to reach
  40 Nm implies ~1.9 kg of actuator plus high-voltage electronics at the joint.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no humans)
- **Limitations:** 6–9 kV at a joint a frail person may grab. >2000 cycles is not a service
  life. Specific torque excludes driver mass, which dominates at this scale.

### Canonical HASEL — Acome et al., 2018

- **Citation:** Acome, E., Mitchell, S. K., Morrissey, T. G., Emmett, M. B., Benjamin, C. E.,
  King, M., Radakovitz, M., Keplinger, C. (2018). "Hydraulically amplified self-healing
  electrostatic actuators with muscle-like performance." *Science* 359(6371), 61–65.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aao6139 — verified via the Semantic Scholar Graph
  API DOI record (title, authors, venue, volume 359, pages 61–65, year 2018).
- **Open access:** no
- **What it shows:** The founding HASEL paper: electrostatic Maxwell stress pumps a liquid
  dielectric to produce large-strain, self-healing, muscle-like actuation.
- **Hard numbers:** Single-unit actuator 107% linear strain under a **250 g load**; two-unit
  actuator 124% linear strain under a **700 g load**. Peak specific power during contraction
  614 W/kg, specific work 70 J/kg (two-unit), ~2× natural muscle peak specific power.
  (Cross-checked against Rothemund 2021 and Tynan 2025, which report the same values; the
  Science page itself is paywalled to automated fetch.)
- **Design relevance:** The headline "muscle-like" claim that will come up in any design review —
  and the numbers that deflate it for this application. 700 g of load is ~7 N. Cite this with
  the load figure attached, not the power-density figure.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no humans)
- **Limitations:** Load figures are grams, not tens of newtons. Liquid dielectric plus ~20 kV
  inside an assistive device that contacts skin is a hazard analysis the team does not want.

### Where the power-density gap actually sits — Kim et al., 2025

- **Citation:** Kim, T., Dunn, E. A., Chen, M., Truby, R. L. (2025). "Architected Soft Actuators
  for Artificial Musculoskeletal Systems." *Advanced Materials* 37(43), e2501290.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202501290 — verified via PMC full text
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12574648/).
- **Open access:** yes (PMC12574648)
- **What it shows:** Low-voltage motorised-tendon soft actuators built into architected
  musculoskeletal assemblies, with an unusually candid comparison against muscle — including the
  authors admitting their own actuators sit an order of magnitude below muscle.
- **Hard numbers:** Actuators: 29.5% linear strain, **75 N max force**, 22.8 W/kg peak power
  density (pulling), ~3.7 J/kg peak energy density, **<10 V drive**. Quoted directly:
  *"The maximum power and energy densities of muscle (200 W kg⁻¹ and 40 J kg⁻¹) are still an
  order of magnitude higher than our actuators' (40 W kg⁻¹ and ≈4 J kg⁻¹)."* Authors also note
  that once auxiliary hardware mass is counted, motorised actuators beat other soft actuators'
  power and energy density by four orders of magnitude.
- **Design relevance:** Two things the team should take: (1) the honest muscle-vs-soft-actuator
  gap number to quote — one order of magnitude on power, ten on energy; (2) the
  auxiliary-hardware argument, which is the strongest technical case for a motor-plus-compliance
  (SEA/VSA) architecture over fully soft. Once you count the compressor, tank, valves or the kV
  driver, "soft" actuators lose their power-density advantage outright.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no humans)
- **Limitations:** 75 N is a tendon force, not a joint torque; no shoulder-scale demonstration.
  Comparative claims rest on supplementary tables not reproduced in the main text.

### SMA reality check — Shami, Arslan & Lomax, 2025

- **Citation:** Shami, Z., Arslan, T., Lomax, P. (2025). "Wearable Soft Robots: Case Study of
  Using Shape Memory Alloys in Rehabilitation." *Bioengineering* 12(3), 276.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12030276 — verified via PMC full text
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11939777/).
- **Open access:** yes (MDPI, CC-BY)
- **What it shows:** A tabulated review of every significant SMA-actuated rehabilitation device,
  with force, cooling time, current draw and efficiency for each. It is the fastest way to
  conclude that SMA is not viable here.
- **Hard numbers:** 0.38 mm NiTi wire: 22.06 N (22.5 N heating / 9 N cooling), 2.2 A drive.
  SMA spring 0.25 N each; 3 in parallel 0.75 N. Hand exoskeleton 10 N per fingertip, 40 N grip.
  AAFO 8-wire 70 N. Platform ankle robot 110 N max force. SHADE ankle 13 N/actuator, 200 N·cm
  (**2 Nm**) max torque; Leia ankle 100 N·cm (**1 Nm**). Nitinol max contraction 4.5% (wires
  3–5%); springs >100%; knitted actuators >40%. Stress limits 266–300 MPa. **Cooling: 2.0 s
  (0.15 mm wire) to 16.8 s (0.51 mm wire) at 70 °C; 40–50 s for a knee orthosis on natural
  convection; 3.57 s with forced convection.** Actuation frequency 0.143–0.5 Hz. Efficiency
  **10–15%**. Work density 10 J/cm³ (25× electric motors). Power draw e.g. 28.8 W for a knee+
  ankle device; 10.2 A at 14.7 V for a fabric muscle.
- **Design relevance:** Eliminates SMA. Highest joint torque anyone has published in a wearable
  SMA device is 2 Nm, at 0.14–0.5 Hz, at 10–15% efficiency, with cooling times measured in tens
  of seconds. A torso a person leans on needs to hold load continuously — which for SMA means
  continuous heating at ~10% efficiency, i.e. a hot device against skin.
- **Evidence grade:** D (review; underlying devices are A/B)
- **Limitations:** Review, not primary data. All devices are wearables with the load path
  through the human skeleton.

### Twisted/coiled polymer — the canonical claim — Haines et al., 2014

- **Citation:** Haines, C. S., Lima, M. D., Li, N., Spinks, G. M., Foroughi, J., Madden, J. D. W.,
  Kim, S. H., Fang, S., Jung de Andrade, M., Göktepe, F., Göktepe, Ö., Mirvakili, S. M.,
  Naficy, S., Lepró, X., Oh, J., Kozlov, M. E., Kim, S. J., Xu, X., Swedlove, B. J.,
  Wallace, G. G., Baughman, R. H. (2014). "Artificial Muscles from Fishing Line and Sewing
  Thread." *Science* 343(6173), 868–872.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1246906 — verified via the Semantic Scholar Graph
  API DOI record including full abstract. Green OA copy on figshare.
- **Open access:** partial (green OA, CC-BY, figshare)
- **What it shows:** Twisting commodity nylon fibre past the coiling point produces a thermally
  driven tensile actuator with very large stroke and extreme specific work. The origin of every
  "artificial muscle from fishing line" headline.
- **Hard numbers:** 49% contraction; lifts loads **>100× heavier than human muscle of the same
  length and weight**; **5.3 kW/kg mechanical work per kg of muscle**; long cycle life,
  non-hysteretic, scalable by twist insertion.
- **Design relevance:** Sets the aspirational upper bound for TCP, and is the number a reviewer
  will quote at the team. The honest counter is in the next entry: per-actuator absolute force
  and bandwidth, not specific work, are what a shoulder needs.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no humans)
- **Limitations:** Thermally driven — heating and cooling set the bandwidth, and cooling is
  uncontrolled. 5.3 kW/kg is a *peak* figure at resonance-like conditions, not sustained
  load-holding. No joint, no robot, no load path.

### Twisted/coiled polymer — what it actually delivers today — Sun et al., 2025

- **Citation:** Sun, J., Zhang, S., Deng, J., Li, J., Zhou, D., Wang, D., Liu, J., Chen, W.,
  Liu, Y. (2025). "High-Performance Twisted Nylon Actuators for Soft Robots." *Research* 8, 0642.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.34133/research.0642 — verified via PMC full text
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11912876/) and the NCBI eSummary record for
  PMID 40099267 (confirms 2025, vol 8, pages 0642).
- **Open access:** yes (PMC11912876)
- **What it shows:** A current best-effort attempt to push twisted nylon actuators toward useful
  absolute force via parallel-twisted and untwisted-parallel architectures, with the structural
  trade-off stated explicitly.
- **Hard numbers:** PTNA (parallel-twisted) **11.0 N output force**, 12.1% contraction under
  15 N load (≈10,000× its own weight), 20.3% contraction at 9 N, 0.25 s heating cycle.
  PTUNA 3.67 N, 53.7% contraction at 2 N. TUNA 60.2% contraction (vertical), ~100% horizontal,
  ~10 s to reach 110 °C, 0.5 N load. Five TUNAs launched a ball to 131 cm in 0.05 s (4.0 m/s).
  Stated trade-off: *"increasing the diameter enhances deformation but also reduces stiffness,
  thereby decreasing the output force."*
- **Design relevance:** Eliminates TCP. Eleven newtons from a state-of-the-art 2025 bundle, with
  a thermal actuation cycle, against a 10–100 N end-effector requirement and a shoulder joint.
  The quoted diameter/stiffness trade-off explains why this family does not scale up by making
  the actuator bigger.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no humans)
- **Limitations:** Thermal, so bandwidth and duty cycle are poor, and sustained load-holding
  means sustained heating. No joint, no assistive demonstration, no safety analysis.

### Low-voltage DEA — the best case for dielectric elastomers — Peng et al., 2026

- **Citation:** Peng, J.-B., Zhuo, J.-S., Qiu, H., Normahmedov, O., Shi, M.-K., Dong, H.-F.,
  Wang, L., Jiang, S.-C., Zou, J., Gu, G.-Y., Li, T.-F., Fu, W.-F., Peng, B., Ma, H.-Z., Shi, Y.
  (2026). "Low-voltage and high-output dielectric elastomer actuators for untethered soft
  machines working at 200 volts." *Science Robotics* 11(111), eady9635.
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.ady9635 — verified via the Semantic Scholar
  Graph API DOI record including full abstract.
- **Open access:** no
- **What it shows:** A new high-permittivity elastomer plus dry-stacked multilayering drops DEA
  drive voltage from kilovolts to 200 V while keeping useful energy and power density — the
  single biggest obstacle to DEAs in human-contact devices, directly addressed.
- **Hard numbers:** **38.4 J/kg energy density, 452 W/kg power density at 20 V/µm nominal field**,
  no prestretch, no resonance. Operation demonstrated at **200 V**. Untethered wearable devices
  and soft robots built and run.
- **Design relevance:** The only credible path by which DEAs become safe enough to sit in a torso
  a person touches. Worth tracking, not worth designing around for a senior design cycle: the
  paper reports energy/power density, not force or torque, so there is no number the team can
  put in a torque budget. Note 38.4 J/kg is still below muscle's 40 J/kg maximum.
- **Evidence grade:** B (hardware-validated, no humans)
- **Limitations:** No force or torque figure reported at all — this is the single most important
  omission for this project. Multilayer dry stacking at scale is a manufacturing research
  problem. Published 2026; no independent replication.

## Verdict for the design team

No genuinely soft actuator technology reaches a 10–40 Nm shoulder, and only one family gets
close enough to argue about. Every electroactive option is out by two to three orders of
magnitude: the highest joint torque ever published from an electrohydraulic soft joint is
70 mN·m (Kellaris 2021), the highest force from any HASEL-family actuator ever is ~60 N from a
multi-unit stack at 12 kV (Tynan 2025), and the DEA state of the art (Peng 2026) reports no
force figure at all — it reports 38.4 J/kg, still under muscle's 40 J/kg. SMA tops out at 2 Nm in
a published wearable at 10–15% efficiency with 3–17 s cooling and 0.14–0.5 Hz bandwidth (Shami
2025); twisted/coiled polymer tops out at 11 N per bundle and gets weaker as you make it bigger
(Sun 2025). Both are thermal, which means holding a static gravity load means holding a heater
against a frail person's arm — that is a disqualifying failure mode, not a tuning problem.
Fluidic soft actuation is the only family in range: purpose-built soft pneumatic shoulder
actuators deliver 5.5 Nm (Zhang 2024) and 9.7–10.9 Nm at 90 kPa (Chen 2026, 10 human subjects),
and a bundled soft pneumatic platform reached 18 Nm at 2 bar (Robertson 2017) — so the bottom of
the target band is reachable with all-soft pneumatics, the top of it is not, and the price is a
compressor, valves, 1.3–2 Hz bandwidth and 2–6 s settling times (Zuo 2025) that are far too slow
to catch someone falling. The architecture that actually clears 10–40 Nm is the soft-rigid
hybrid: 400 N TPU pneumatic muscles at 240 kPa on a rigid antagonistic frame, with measured
stiffness modulation of 0.054–0.076 Nm/deg (Dimonte 2026), which is 20–40 Nm on a 50–100 mm
moment arm. One further caveat the team must not skip: every torque number above comes from a
*wearable*, where the human skeleton carries the reaction load — a freestanding torso must
generate that torque **and** react a leaning adult's body weight through its own structure, and
no paper in this set characterises that case, which pushes the real problem into B1 (structure)
as much as A1 (actuation). Plan on a hybrid, spend the softness budget on the contact surfaces
and the outer 100 mm of the arm, and treat "fully soft arm" as a claim the evidence does not
support.

## Adjacent topics I hit that my assignment did not cover

- **Auxiliary-hardware mass is the real power-density argument (→ A2).** Kim et al. 2025 note
  that once you count compressors, tanks, valves and kV drivers, motorised actuators beat soft
  actuators on power and energy density by four orders of magnitude. That is the strongest
  technical case for SEA/VSA and belongs in A2, not here.
- **Variable stiffness as a design variable, not a material property (→ A2/B1).** Bruder et al.
  2023 (localized stiffening) and Dimonte et al. 2026 (antagonistic pressure → measured
  0.054–0.076 Nm/deg) both treat stiffness as something you schedule. A2 should pick this up.
- **Self-regulated McKibben actuators with embedded valves (→ A3).** Zuo et al. 2025 eliminate
  external regulators entirely. Also the untethered runtime figure — 11 min 45 s from a 1.11 L
  310 bar tank — which is an A3-level plumbing result with hard consequences for the torso.
- **Negative-pressure (vacuum) actuation as an intrinsic safety property (→ C1/C2).** Li et al.
  2017 make the point that vacuum-driven muscles fail safe in a way positive-pressure fluidics
  do not. That is a safety-case argument, not an actuation one.
- **ISO/TS 15066-grounded variable impedance control (→ C1/D1).** Surfaced repeatedly:
  arXiv:2311.13814, "Safe Physical Human-Robot Interaction through Variable Impedance Control
  based on ISO/TS 15066". Not verified in depth — handing to C1/D1.
- **"Soft is Safe: Human-Robot Interaction for Soft Robots" (arXiv:2502.01256) (→ C2/E2).**
  Appeared in two searches; looks like a direct challenge to the assumption that soft
  automatically equals safe. Worth someone's time in C2.
- **Outcome measures are shifting away from torque (→ E1/E2).** *eBioMedicine* 121, 106031
  (2025), "Embodiment as outcome: the translational test for soft robotics" (verified via
  PMC12790142), argues explicitly that "raw torque is no longer enough" and that embodiment,
  home validation, retention and abandonment are the benchmarks. This should shape E1's spec,
  and it is a warning that a purely force-based project narrative will read as dated.
- **Electrofluidic fiber muscles, *Science Robotics*, doi:10.1126/scirobotics.ady6438** —
  appeared in search with ~50 W/kg power density, 20% contraction, 0.3 s response. **Not
  verified in depth.** Possible A1 addendum if someone confirms it.
- **UNVERIFIED NUMBER — do not cite.** A search summary attributed "peak deltoid torque to fully
  elevate the arm = 43 Nm" to the shoulder-exosuit literature. I fetched arXiv:2602.18212 in full
  and that figure is **not in it**, and I could not trace it to any paper. The 10–40 Nm design
  target needs a real biomechanics citation from E1 before it goes in a spec table.
